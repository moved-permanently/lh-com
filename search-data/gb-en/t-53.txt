["Plan your trip to Vilnius with ease: take the train from the airport to the city, plus tips on the JUHU Card, opening hours, language, and red lights.","Vilnius is the capital of Lithuania and, with over 600,000 inhabitants, the largest city in the Baltic region. Here you’ll find Baroque churches, creative venues, and plenty of green spaces packed into a compact area. The city is easy to get to know, yet reveals many of its details only on second glance. We’ve summarized here what you should know before your stay Vilnius International Airport (VNO) is located just a few miles south of the city center. The regional train is particularly convenient, taking around 8 minutes to reach the main station and costing about 1 euro. Bus routes 1 and 2 also offer an affordable way to get into the city center. A ticket costs around 1 euro and can be purchased via an app, using a JUHU Card, or by credit card on the bus or at a ticket machine. Taxis are available right outside the terminal; the journey to the Old Town costs between 20 and 30 euros, depending on traffic. The center of Vilnius is easy to explore on foot, with many neighborhoods blending seamlessly into one another. The Old Town can be comfortably explored without additional transport, as numerous cafés, galleries, and sights are within walking distance of one another. Bicycles and e-scooters are widely available in summer and are used to complement public transport. The city also has a dense network of buses and trolleybuses, so a car is rarely needed within the city center. Shops usually open between 9 a.m. and 10 a.m. and close by 9 p.m. at the latest. Cafés often open early in the day, while restaurants usually stay open until 10 p.m. or later in the evening. Supermarkets are open daily in many places, including on Sundays. On Mondays, some smaller galleries or museums are closed. Lithuania regularly scores well in international English rankings. Nevertheless, knowing a few words in Lithuanian is a nice gesture: “Laba diena.” (La-ba di-ena) – Good day. “Ačiū.” (A-choo) – Thank you. “Kur yra stotis?” (Kur yi-ra sto-tis) – Where is the station? In Vilnius, you shouldn’t ignore red lights at pedestrian crossings. Even when the streets are empty, you’re expected to wait until the light turns green. Crossing the road on red attracts unwanted attention and breaches social norms. Furthermore, fines may be imposed if pedestrians ignore red lights. On summer evenings, hot-air balloons float over the Old Town, which is one of the few UNESCO-protected sites worldwide where such flights are permitted. The stable wind conditions make it the ideal location, and the launch sites are just a few miles from the city center.
