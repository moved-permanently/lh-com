["Discover the best activities in Marseille: from bouillabaisse at the Old Port to Notre-Dame de la Garde and hikes in the Calanques.","The French coastal city of Marseille has plenty to offer its visitors. Here you’ll find inspiration for which culinary delights to savor, what you absolutely must see, and what makes a visit to France’s second-largest city so special Marseille is best known for its lively Vieux-Port (Old Port), where both boats and seafood restaurants line the quayside. It will come as no surprise that the traditional delicacy bouillabaisse is a particularly common dish here. What was originally a simple dish made from leftovers has long since become an opulent meal. The fish stew is served in two courses: first as a rich, aromatic soup, garnished with crispy toasted croutons and the spicy garlic and chilli sauce known as rouille. This is followed by six or seven varieties of fine Mediterranean fish, complemented by vegetables, herbs, and saffron. Anyone wishing to experience Marseille with all their senses should definitely try this culinary icon – ideally accompanied by a chilled glass of Cassis Blanc from Provence or a refreshing non-alcoholic citron pressé, whose zesty citrus notes perfectly balance the strong fish and saffron flavors. (© Mauritius; header image © Getty Images) More Culinary Delights: Panisse de l’Estaque: This snack made from chickpea flour, crisp on the outside and creamy on the inside, is considered a true vegetarian classic and is traditionally sold at small snack bars in the L’Estaque district in the north of Marseille. Oursins: The golden-yellowy orange-colored roe of the sea urchin is one of the most prized delicacies of southern French seafood cuisine. Its oyster-like flavor, accompanied by a salty-sweet, nutty note, is complemented by a delicate, creamy texture that melts in the mouth. It is best served raw – with a squeeze of lemon and some bread. Poutargue: What looks like a flat, brick-red bar is considered the caviar of the Mediterranean: poutargue is a delicacy made from salted, pressed, and dried mullet roe, offering an explosion of flavor that combines a delicate umami aroma reminiscent of Parmesan with a spicy, salty, and iodine-rich hint of the sea. Anyone wishing to catch a superb panoramic view of Marseille should definitely visit the Basilique Notre-Dame de la Garde. The city spreads out like a model at the foot of the limestone cliff on which the church is built, and your gaze is drawn from the Old Port to the Frioul Islands. With its model ships and votive offerings, the interior of this Marseille landmark vividly illustrates the city’s connection to seafaring. It feels like a quiet, sheltered contrast to the hustle and bustle of Marseille’s city center. (© Getty Images) Also Worth Seeing: Le Panier: Marseille’s oldest district has existed since ancient times and forms the origins of the French Mediterranean metropolis. This enchanting neighborhood, situated on a hill in the city center, is reminiscent of a village in Provence, with narrow, winding alleys, street art and graffiti adorning colorful building façades, and small, quiet squares. Mucem: At the Old Port and right by the water, a striking glass cube with a delicate, shade-providing fishing net façade punctuates the Marseille skyline. Inside is the Museum of European and Mediterranean Civilizations – or Mucem for short. On top is a roof terrace affording sweeping views of the sea, the port, and Fort Saint-Jean. Château d’If: The former 16th-century fortress and later prison island looks like a Mediterranean Alcatraz off the coast of Marseille, where political and religious prisoners were once held. However, it gained fame through Alexandre Dumas’ novel “The Count of Monte Cristo.” Even today, designated cells and references to the heroic figure of Edmond Dantès serve as a reminder, making the place a blend of true history and literary myth. Marseille isn't just about cuisine and culture, it also has an adventurous side. Narrow hiking trails in the Calanques National Park wind along the limestone cliffs between Marseille and Cassis, leading to fjord-like bays, pine-covered slopes, and small pebble beaches where the water glows a heavenly turquoise. This hike combines unique sea views, soothing tranquillity, and Mediterranean vegetation to form a stunning natural experience right outside the city. (© Getty Images) More Experiences: Kayak or stand-up paddle tour in the Calanques: From the water, the rock faces of the Calanques appear even more imposing as you glide close to the cliffs in a kayak, and small grottoes, swimming coves, and rocky outcrops come into view. Walk along the Corniche Kennedy: This road runs high above the sea along the south coast of Marseille, connecting small city beaches, rocky coves, and bathing spots and offering sweeping views of the Frioul Islands. Market experience in the Noailles district: Around the street market of the Noailles district, stalls selling fresh vegetables, fruit, herbs, and spices from across the Mediterranean are packed closely together. Typically, goat meat is slowly grilled for Nyama Choma and then served with ugali (maize porridge) and kachumbari (tomato and onion salad) – traditionally on a wooden platter from which all guests can help themselves. This ritual is celebrated with particular authenticity at The Carnivore restaurant, a firm fixture of Kenya’s barbecue culture scene since 1980. (© Mauritius/Alamy; Header-Bild © Shutterstock) More Culinary Delights: Mandazi: These slightly sweetened dough balls, often flavored with coconut milk and spiced with cardamom, look like triangular doughnuts. They are deep-fried until golden brown and are often served for breakfast or with tea. Tilapia: Tilapia, a cichlid, is usually served whole in Kenyan cuisine, either baked in the oven or crispy fried with sauce. The Mama Oliech restaurant is considered a mecca for anyone who enjoys freshwater fish dishes. Sukuma Wiki: Literally translated, the name Sukuma Wiki means “stretching the week,” which hints at just how affordable and filling this traditional East African dish is. It consists of finely chopped collard greens sautéed with onions, tomatoes, and spices, and is usually served with ugali, a thick maize porridge.
