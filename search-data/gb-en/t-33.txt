Mangoes for the Angels Bangkok isn't the city's only name. Its official name is much harder to pronounce, and, at around 168 Latin characters, it's the longest city name in the world. The Buddha's New Clothes The spiritual heart of Thailand is Wat Phra Kaew, the Temple of the Emerald Buddha. The temple's namesake statue, carved from jade, is the country's national shrine and stands just 66 centimeters tall. Three times a year, the tiny green statue receives a new robe, with different outfits provided for the rainy, hot, and cool seasons. Royal Fruit Banned Bangkok offers an incredible variety of fresh fruit, including mangoes, melons, and pineapples. Everything is sweet, juicy, and delicious. But there is one exception: the infamous durian. Although it's considered a delicacy and nicknamed the \"queen of fruits,\" it has a very unusual smell, reminiscent of onions and rotten eggs. Construction began in 1997 but came to a halt during the Asian financial crisis, leaving behind an eerie, unfinished skyscraper. Students, families, and visitors come together in a relaxed way. ["Discover the best things to do in Zagreb at a glance, with tips on food, sights, and experiences for an inspiring stay.","Between magnificent façades from the Habsburg era, elegant parks, and a distinctive coffee house culture, Zagreb shows a Central European side of Croatia that surprises many. At the same time, the city exudes a relaxed, Southeast European joie de vivre that still characterizes squares, markets, and street cafés today. It is precisely this mix of elegance and serenity that makes Zagreb so special Visually, Štrukli is a little reminiscent of börek or stuffed strudel dishes, but in terms of taste, this specialty goes in a much creamier direction. Thin dough is filled with cream cheese, sour cream, and egg, then served boiled or baked golden brown – traditionally vegetarian. It is the interplay of delicate shell and warm, airy interior that makes it so appealing. In Zagreb, Štrukli is therefore considered less of a snack and more of a real comfort food with cult status. (© Shutterstock; header image © Getty Images) More Culinary Delights: Burek at Dolac Market: Under Zagreb's famous red umbrellas, a fresh burek tastes particularly delicious. The crispy baked dough specialty with a meat, cheese, or spinach filling was brought to the Balkans by the Ottomans and is now an integral part of the city's fast-paced everyday life. Warm from the oven, it is both a hearty breakfast classic and late night soul food. Kremšnita for a coffee break: The combination of puff pastry and vanilla cream results in one of the most popular desserts in the city. The Kremšnita combines sweetness with an airy lightness and thus fits perfectly with Zagreb's coffee house culture. Served chilled, it looks surprisingly elegant – a slice of Central Europe on a cake plate. Zagrebački odrezak, the hearty classic: Behind the name is a breaded schnitzel, traditionally filled with ham and cheese – ultimately Zagreb's version of a cordon bleu. Crispy on the outside, juicy and molten on the inside. A few steps uphill are enough for you to suddenly see Zagreb as a different city. In Gornji Grad, the historic Upper Town, cobblestones, baroque façades, and quiet alleys meet viewpoints with sweeping views over the rooftops of the Lower Town. The focal point is St. Mark's Church with its colorful tiled roof, which shows the coats of arms of Zagreb and Croatia and is one of the most striking motifs of the city. (© Getty Images) Also Worth Seeing: Museum of Broken Relationships: Lovesickness as museum material sounds unusual at first – that's exactly what makes this place so special. In a baroque palace in the Upper Town, personal objects from all over the world tell of separations, hopes, and memories. Sometimes humorous, sometimes touching, and often surprisingly honest, it is one of the most original museum experiences in Europe. Zagreb Cathedral: Few other buildings shape Zagreb as much as the cathedral with its striking towers. As the most important sacred building in the country and one of the tallest church buildings in Europe southeast of the Alps, it is visible far beyond the city. It is this presence that makes it both a point of orientation and a symbol of Zagreb. Zrinjevac Park: If Zagreb ever looks elegant, it's often here. The historic park with a fountain, music pavilion, rows of old trees, and classicist façades all around is part of the famous Lenuci horseshoe complex from the 19th century. It's the ideal place to take a break and experience how green and stylish a capital can be. In Zagreb, coffee is not just drunk – it is celebrated. On Tkalčićeva ulica in particular, often simply called Tkalča by locals, there is any number of terraces, bars, and cafés. Where glasses clink and waiters weave among tables today, a stream used to run between the historic towns of Gradec and Kaptol. If you take a seat here, you will experience Zagreb's relaxed side and get the feeling that time passes slower here for a moment.
