["Nemo Vesper, who comes from Hamburg, has been working as a freelance booker for bars and restaurants in Shanghai since 2017. He told us about his experiences there.","Nemo Vesper grew up in Hamburg, Germany, the son of a Chinese mother and a German father. The nightlife professional has been living in Shanghai since 2017. What fascinates him most? The speed and the lights Even as a child, I loved Shanghai because it was so different from Hamburg, where I grew up. And it’s my mom’s home – so moving here has also meant getting to know my Chinese side. I’ve learned that I feel very comfortable in both worlds. Shanghai has more of everything. It’s bigger, louder, more colorful, and faster than other major cities. When you’re thundering along the highways in a taxi at sunset and the neon signs on the skyscrapers light up, it’s an incomparable feeling. Even after all these years. Expats from all over the world mix here, including many Asians from the US and Canada. This means you’ll meet new people almost every day, giving you a different perspective on life. I don’t just work in nightlife, I love it too. The countless bars and clubs and great restaurants – in every price range – are simply unique. The people you meet are extremely open and hospitable. And you get to go out a lot more here than in other cities I’ve been to. Shanghai is, of course, best known for its exciting and modern architecture. But there is also a very different side to discover here. I always take visitors to the French Concession, the old French quarter. It has a wonderful retro charm and is full of cafés and boutiques. Don’t miss a day trip to Zhujiajiao. The many waterways and bridges have earned this historic city the nickname “Venice of China.” My favorite place for dinner in Shanghai is the Lotus Eatery – super exciting southern Chinese cuisine. ["Photographer Cheriss May moved to Washington, D.C., for her studies. Now, she reveals what makes the city and its residents so unique.","Photographer Cheriss May grew up in Kansas City, Missouri, and moved to Washington, D.C., for her studies at Howard University. There, she got to know people who speak their minds, dance to music she was previously unfamiliar with, and attend shows on the former \"Black Broadway\" The People The District of Columbia, as Washington, D.C., is officially called, is not a state, which is why its residents do not enjoy all the benefits that come with statehood. Despite or perhaps because of this shortcoming, the people of Washington are very resilient. They are ambitious, successful, but also have a social conscience and political awareness. What I particularly like is that Washingtonians are not afraid to speak their minds unapologetically. The Inspiration I love the convergence of the many cultures. In the spring and summer, there are numerous festivals of different groups celebrating their community and cultural heritage. It teaches me a lot about people, and for me as a photographer, it's important to make authentic connections and develop an understanding of other cultures and experiences. Because that helps me to become a better storyteller. Imposing: the Washington Monument rises into the blue sky behind the National Museum of African American History and Culture (© Alan Karchmer; header image © Per Bernal) From string trios to pop and heavy metal, anything goes at The Anthem concert hall (© Cheriss May) Howard University: many prominent African-Americans have studied here, including Kamala Harris (© Cheriss May) \"What I particularly like is that Washingtonians are not afraid to speak their minds unapologetically.\" Cheriss May The Music Washington, D.C., is not only the capital of the US, but also of go-go music, a mix of funk, R&B, and soul with many percussion instruments such as congas and cowbells. It's strongly rooted in African musical traditions. The almost hypnotic rhythms, which never let up, and the driving bass line are impossible for listeners to resist. Go-go feels like a conversation between band and audience – with call and response, it brings people together. I remember the first time I experienced a go-go concert as a young student; it was electrifying, and I was captivated and a new fan! The Essence Washington, D.C., is an almost mythical place, the center of power in the country, but it is also a place where normal life takes place outside of politics. The city is characterized by the tension between symbolic charge and normal everyday life. Sometimes, I wait at a traffic light, look up, see one of the many monuments, and can't believe it's real – and that I get to live here. For Friends I love my alma mater, Howard University, so I would always recommend visitors to go on campus to The Yard. It's an HBCU, historically Black college and university, where you can follow in the footsteps of iconic alumni like Thurgood Marshall, the first Black Supreme Court justice, Nobel and Pulitzer Prize-winning novelist Toni Morrison, and my former next-door neighbor Chadwick Boseman, who was an award-winning actor and producer. A regular stop for me is Busboys and Poets on 14th and V Street. I come here for brunch with friends and for poetry slams, art exhibitions, and film screenings. Of course, there are also many museums in D.C. that you should see: the National Portrait Gallery and the National Museum of African American History and Culture, for example. One cool place to visit is Dupont Underground, an exhibition space in a disused subway station that is run voluntarily by citizens and provides a forum for up-and-coming artists, often people of color. Catch a show at the Lincoln Theatre. At the time of segregation, the theater was the epicenter of Black culture and art on U Street, which is why it was also known as \"Black Broadway.\" Another great spot for a show: The Anthem in the Southwest Waterfront neighborhood. What I came to know that's fascinating: D.C. is home to the largest Ethiopian community in the United States. One of my favorite places to enjoy the cuisine is Dukem. Speaking of food, there's a D.C. staple that is much more than a condiment, but more so embedded in the cultural fabric of the community – Mumbo sauce, a sweet and savory tomato-based sauce that the residents of the US capital eat on a lot of things. It's a staple much like hot sauce and barbecue sauce is to others. About Cheriss May is a portrait and editorial photographer in Washington, D.C., and Los Angeles. ["Germany and the team are looking forward to the European Championship: Optimism prevails thanks to a mixture of experienced and young players and a new sense of unity.","In Germany, as well as within the national team, excitement for the European Championship is growing – and with it a hushed sense that something special is brewing! Why? Because of the fine balance between experience and freshness in the team, and a new sense of unity There is a special air of anticipation around the German national team ahead of this summer’s European Championships, set on home turf across 10 German cities. Long gone are the echoes of disappointment from the 2022 World Cup group stage exit. Instead, there is a new sense of joy and unity in the team under new national coach Julian Nagelsmann and his assistants Sandro Wagner and Benjamin Glück. Many things have changed, not least the way the team now sees and presents itself as a strong group. Looking ahead to the first games in June, this team is a close-knit unit. Deniz Undav's family advised him to enjoy his time with the national team Coach Nagelsmann's mission for Maximilian Beier: be cheeky and annoy the opposition Playing for the national team is a childhood dream come true for Niclas Füllkrug One new player of this up-and-coming generation is Deniz Undav, one of the biggest surprises in this year's Bundesliga season. The 27-year-old striker, who is on loan to Stuttgart from Brighton & Hove Albion, is one of the latest additions to Nagelsmann's squad and made his debut in a friendly against France in March. For him, being able to give something back to the fans is one of the greatest gifts a footballer can receive. \"My family told me to enjoy my time with the national team,\" says Deniz. \"I think it's important to stay true to yourself and not let it change you.\" For the German-Turkish newcomer, it's ultimately about giving something back to the fans: \"The team wants to repay the fans with good performances.\" Alongside Undav, the German attack includes Niclas Füllkrug, who found his way into the squad ahead of the 2022 World Cup in Qatar. \"As the team grows, every player learns how they can have a positive influence on the group,\" says Niclas. “As a kid, the national team is the greatest thing there is. It's the biggest dream you can achieve as a footballer,\" he emphasizes. \"Everyone in this group, even those who don't have as many titles, can bring different experiences to the table. Even off the pitch, because they all had to fight for their careers in different ways. That's all very valuable.\" Youngster Maximilian Beier, who is currently making waves at TSG 1899 Hoffenheim, is another newcomer who is now looking for his way to contribute to the team. Even without years of experience, his role is clear, at least in the eyes of Nagelsmann. \"I have been told I should be cheeky. I should prove myself and make life as difficult as possible for the others on the pitch. So I don't feel any pressure. I'm just looking forward to being here and being able to train with the guys.\" \"The team wants to repay the fans with good performances.\" Deniz Undav Returning world champion Toni Kroos knows what a team needs to win But youthful enthusiasm and new energies alone won’t win titles. Returning to the national team is Toni Kroos, the old and new number 8, who has already demonstrated his incredible calm and charisma in the heart of the midfield in recent test matches. Kroos has already achieved everything, most recently winning the Champions League once more with Real Madrid and thus crowning the end of his club career. He is aware of the responsibility and knows how his own successes can help others. \"Winning the World Cup changes your life. It's the greatest thing there is. Nevertheless, I try to push others forward and make it clear that everyone has to take responsibility. But that only works if you act as a group.\" Another key player is Joshua Kimmich, who has won more titles in recent years than most players do in their entire career. The FC Bayern Munich all-rounder knows the taste of victory well and wants to share it with his younger team-mates as a leader. \"You need courage to achieve something special and you have to be able to believe in yourself,\" he says. \"You learn that with experience. But above all, you have to believe that you are capable of helping the group wherever you can.\" Joshua Kimmich, FC Bayern's versatile star, is also indispensable for Germany \"You need courage to achieve something special and you have to be able to believe in yourself.\" Joshua Kimmich Another important part of the story of the new national team are players who know the team inside out, but also know how important it is to adapt and grow as part of a unit. Jonathan Tah, for example, can already look back on a long track record of success. He made his way through the youth ranks at Hamburger SV before being called up to the national team. With his current club Bayer 04 Leverkusen, Tah recently became Bundesliga champion and German Cup winner. Now that he has established himself as a player and in private life, he is also ready to take on more responsibility. \"It's important to stay grounded, no matter what. You need a base with your family that you can return to and that allows you to stay true to yourself, in good times and bad,\" he says. \"I want to take on responsibility now and make sure that our team makes a difference.\" Jonathan Tah's family keeps him grounded (all images © Leon Schleßelmann / DFB x Lufthansa; header image © DFB/Philipp Reinhard) As the tournament gets underway in Germany, the dream of making a difference will continue to grow for Tah and everyone else in and around the team. And – no less importantly – for the fans too. Everyone now agrees that Nagelsmann's new ideas are creating a special spirit. And that gives hope that something special can happen. Slowly but surely, observers everywhere are sensing a new spirit surrounding the German team. The key to success lies in having a common goal, and in creating a togetherness that goes beyond tactics and theory. With a series of good results in spring 2024, the effects are now being felt on the pitch. The focus is on a collective vision that will define the new German national team in the future. The recipe is a special blend of talent and experience, adaptability and clear ideas of roles and responsibilities. The ultimate goal is the sum of what each individual can bring to the table: a true unit, on and off the pitch. The summer of 2024 will hopefully be unforgettable for the German national players, the coaches and the fans. And as the national team has shown in recent months after the restart, it is prepared for anything. And it has the right spirit to go with it.
