["Discover the best things to do in Almaty at a glance, with tips on food, sights, and experiences for an inspiring stay.","Nestled at the foot of the majestic Trans-Ili Alatau mountains lies Almaty, the former capital of Kazakhstan. Deeply rooted in the nomadic traditions of its inhabitants, Kazakhstan’s largest city offers a thriving culinary scene and a vibrant urban lifestyle. We’ll show you what makes this destination so surprising with its cultural contrasts against an alpine backdrop Beshbarmak is considered Kazakhstan’s undisputed national dish and literally translates as \"five fingers,\" as it is traditionally eaten with your hands. The hearty combination of tenderly cooked horse or lamb, flat homemade noodles, and a robust onion broth is simply delicious. Modern interpretations of this classic, served against a backdrop of stylish, traditional décor, can be found at the SandyQ restaurant in the historic heart of the city. (© Shutterstock; header image © Getty Images) More Culinary Delights: Manti: These steamed dumplings are a staple of Central Asian cuisine. Filled with spicy minced meat or lentils, they are served with a generous dollop of sour cream and can be found in countless restaurants and eateries across the city. Kazy: This traditional air-dried horse meat sausage is a delicacy that is a must on any festive Kazakh table. Generously seasoned with garlic and pepper, it has a robust flavor and is often served in thin slices as a starter. Baursaki: These small dough pockets, fried in oil until golden brown, are the Kazakh equivalent of bread and a staple of every meal. Slightly crispy on the outside and light and fluffy on the inside, they taste great with hearty stews or on their own with a little honey or jam. In the heart of the sprawling Panfilov Park stands the Ascension Cathedral, one of the tallest Orthodox church buildings in the world constructed entirely of wood. This architectural masterpiece from the early 20th century was built entirely without nails and, thanks to its flexible construction, has survived even severe earthquakes unscathed. The bright yellow façade with its colorful domes forms a fascinating contrast to the austere Soviet buildings in the surrounding area. (© Getty Images) Also Worth Seeing: Green Bazaar (Zelyony Bazaar): This sprawling market, housed in a striking building of Soviet modernist architecture, is the city’s bustling commercial hub. Weaving between pyramid-shaped piles of spices, fresh pomegranate juice, and Korean salads, you can experience the cultural diversity of Central Asia at first hand. Almaty Metro: The city’s underground network may be small, but its architecture is truly impressive. Stations such as Baikonur, with their futuristic design, pay homage to Kazakhstan’s space history, while other stations shine with elaborate mosaics and marble cladding. Kök Töbe: Almaty’s local mountain can be easily reached by cable car directly from the city center. From the viewing platform at the foot of the striking TV tower, a sweeping view opens up over the metropolis’ sparkling sea of lights, particularly in the evening hours. Just a short drive from the city center lies the Medeu Valley, world-famous for its gigantic speed skating rink at an altitude of over 1,600 meters (5,250 feet). Surrounded by dense coniferous forests and snow-capped peaks, the stadium transforms into a huge open-air ice rink in winter, while in summer the valley serves as the ideal starting point for long mountain hikes. Nestled between steep rock faces, this is the perfect place to enjoy the impressive natural beauty of the Central Asian mountains close to the city. (© Getty Images) More Experiences: Relaxation at the Arasan Baths: This expansive bathing complex from the 1980s combines a Russian banya, Finnish sauna, and Turkish hammam beneath striking domed roofs. A visit offers not only deep relaxation but also a fascinating journey back in time to Soviet bathing culture. Trip to the Great Almaty Lake: Situated at an altitude of over 2,500 meters (8,202 feet), this natural reservoir glows in intense shades of turquoise or emerald depending on the season. The rugged mountain landscape surrounding the lake offers a spectacular natural panorama that impressively highlights the region’s alpine character. Stroll along Panfilov Street: This bustling pedestrian zone in the heart of the city is the ideal place to soak up the urban life of Almaty. Here, numerous restaurants and boutiques line the street, musicians provide the musical backdrop, and historic buildings and modern art installations offer charming contrasts. Discovery is NASA's most traveled space shuttle: it spent a total of 365 days in space, orbited Earth 5,830 times, and covered nearly 150 million miles (240 million kilometers). Ottoman palaces, French colonial architecture, and vibrant North African modernism all shape the cityscape in equal measure. Here are our tips for a varied stay In the city’s winding alleys, bourek annabi is one of the undisputed classics of Algerian cuisine. These crispy deep-fried pastry rolls, traditionally filled with spiced minced meat, melted cheese, and egg, as well as fresh herbs, combine Ottoman influences with North African spice. They are a hearty snack that perfectly captures the city’s culinary identity and are best eaten hot, using your hands. They are at their freshest at the bakeries and snack bars around Place des Martyrs. (© Shutterstock; header image © Getty Images) More Culinary Delights: Couscous Algérois: This is a traditional Friday classic. Unlike Moroccan versions, Algerian couscous is often served with a light, gently cinnamon-flavored broth, white turnips, courgettes, and tender lamb. Makroud: This sweet pastry made from semolina dough and date paste is soaked in honey after baking and flavored with orange blossom water. It's an aromatic treat that is a must for any afternoon tea break. Garantita: This is a simple yet iconic street food dish made from chickpea flour, baked as a sort of savory flan. It is often served on a slice of baguette with plenty of cumin and harissa paste. The Jardin d’Essai du Hamma forms a green oasis in the midst of the bustling city and is considered one of the world’s most significant botanical gardens. Among bamboo that's three feet tall, sprawling dragon trees, and French gardens, such an exotic variety of plants is revealed that it even served as the backdrop for a Tarzan film. A stroll through these extensive grounds offers an unexpected, almost tropical perspective on Algiers. Due to the steep topography, the cityscape of Algiers is characterized by a network of cable cars connecting the low-lying coastal districts with the higher residential areas. A ride in the suspended cabins offers not only a welcome shortcut but also a dizzying bird’s-eye view of the sea of white buildings, the winding alleys, and the vast harbor.
