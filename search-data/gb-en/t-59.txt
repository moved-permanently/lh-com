["Join photographer Zac Tan as he explores Singapore and discover temples, neighborhoods, and markets where the spirit of the past is still palpable.","With his camera Zac Tan captures how rapidly his hometown Singapore is changing. In doing so, he continually discovers places where time has stood still and which tell of the city's rich cultural heritage. Here he reveals what fascinates him about the very place he calls home Architecture from the Tang Dynasty The Buddha Tooth Relic Temple boasts a beautifully balanced and symmetrical exterior that stands out against Singapore’s modern cityscape. The architecture is inspired by the Tang Dynasty, one of China's most powerful imperial dynasties, which ruled from 618 to 907 AD. A visit at twilight around 7 p.m., when warm lights shine against the deep blue sky, is particularly atmospheric. A Place of Peace and Calm in the Middle of Chinatown The Buddha Tooth Relic Temple invites its visitors to pause and reflect in the midst of bustling Chinatown. In the magnificent building, which houses what is said to be Buddha's left canine tooth, I enjoy watching the daily rituals of the monks and nuns. You can also explore the upper floors, which house a small museum sharing the history and teachings of Buddhism. Experience the Past and Present on Pagoda Street Pagoda Street lies at the vibrant heart of Chinatown – lively, colorful, and steeped in history. Where craftsmen once pounded their hammers, shops selling souvenirs, handicrafts, and jewelry now line the street. Despite all the modernity, the spirit of the past is still palpable here. The colorful facades of the many shophouses - narrow terraced houses with shops on the ground floor - are particularly reminiscent of the Singapore of yesteryear. In contrast to the city's skyscrapers, they almost look like toy houses. Immerse Yourself in the Colorful Streets of Little India At Buffalo Road in Little India, I'm confronted with so many impressions at once that I often don't know where to look first. The shops sell spices, sweets, saris, and offerings for temple visits, and the air is filled with the sweet scent of the flower garlands that are sold everywhere here. The name of the street comes from the many cows that were once herded through the alleys of the neighborhood. In the past, it was common for the milkman to come directly to your front door with his cow. The colorful Tan Teng Niah House nearby is also well worth visiting. The last remaining Chinese villa in Little India, it was built by a Chinese merchant at the end of the 20th century for his wife. Today, the building and its colorful facade is a popular photo spot. I like to lose myself in this labyrinth of stores, where you can find everything from cuddly toys and colorful saris to delicious kebabs. The city has changed radically in the last twenty years. Cyberabad, as the new IT district in the west is known, is now characterized by office towers and shopping malls. But I find familiarity and comfort, as well as the feeling of being in the Hyderabad I have always known, precisely in the older streets. The Bonalu Festival: Saying Thank You to the Mother Goddess Of the many festivals that are unique to Hyderabad and the surrounding area, Bonalu is perhaps the most important. It began as a festival of thanksgiving to the Mother Goddess in her many manifestations after prayers to her ended a plague epidemic in the 19th century. During the festival, women wear pots of cooked food on their heads and carry them to the local temple. The men dress up as Potharajus, the brother of the goddess Mahakali, and dance with whips to the sound of traditional drums or to the beat of thumping DJ music through the streets. The Khajaguda Hills: The Stony Quirks of Nature Millions of years ago, intense volcanic activity formed the landscape surrounding Hyderabad. What remains are thousands of ancient boulders, many of which have impressive shapes and patterns. The Khajaguda Hills is one of the few places where you can still visit and admire these extraordinary geological formations. A place of prayer and the center of Hyderabad's identity: the Charminar Crowds and unbridled joie de vivre: a street parade in Hyderabad During the Sadar festival, dairy farmers traditionally parade through the city with bulls and buffalo Hyderabad and Festivals: A Real Love Affair The city loves its festivals. Many of these festivities flood the streets with joy, colors, sounds, and movement. Hyderabad is essentially a beautiful mosaic of the many cultural groups that have made it their home, and its festivals and rituals reflect this rich shared heritage. The Sadar festival is traditionally celebrated by the Yadavs, who are Hyderabad's traditional dairy farmers. The highlight is a large parade featuring bulls and buffalo through the city. Laad Bazaar: Glittering Alley at the Charminar Laad Bazaar is famous for its stores selling traditional bangles. As you stroll through the narrow alleyways, you can watch artisans at small workplaces skillfully shaping bangles from lacquer, the main material, as well as metal wire and semi-precious stones. The Longest Table in the World: The Table of the Taj Falaknuma I never had the opportunity to sit at the Taj Falaknuma's famous table, which seats 101 people. But like most Hyderabadis, I'm perfectly content to meet up with friends at one of the many Irani cafés scattered around the city.
