["Lufthansa Manager Stefanie Sauer recommends exploring Helsinki's underground. A surprising world opens up below the surface: with a playground, swimming pool, skate park, and museum A second Helsinki is hidden beneath the streets of the Finnish capital: an extensive network of air-raid shelters that would be ready for use in an emergency, but are currently used quite pragmatically for leisure activities. If you want to find these places hidden below ground, look out for the orange signs with a blue triangle. They indicate that you are heading underground. No daylight? No problem with all the brightly colored slides, climbing frames, and trampolines. Itäkeskus Swimming Hall is a huge swimming pool carved deep into the granite rock. With a kids' pool and waterslides, children have also been thought of here. Adults can look forward to five saunas, the whirlpool, or the Turkish bath. The cool crowd meets in the Luuppi Skate indoor skate park to practice tricks and let off steam underground on the kicker, rail, and halfpipe. Visitors can experience large, immersive exhibitions at Amos Rex. Although the museum is not located in an air raid shelter, it is also underground.
