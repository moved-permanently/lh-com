The Metamorphosis Since I moved here, Dublin has changed a lot. The modern glass buildings in the Docklands didn't exist back then – today, they make the city feel far less like a village. Over the years, Dublin has also become more multicultural. Many people from Spain, Croatia, South America, and other parts of the world have moved here and brought their cultures with them. That has enriched the restaurant scene as well – which is very inspiring for me as a chef. For Friends When friends come to visit, I always tell them to soak up Dublin's pub culture – after all, the city is full of pubs steeped in history. Jonathan Swift, author of \"Gulliver's Travels,\" once sat at the bar of The Brazen Head, Ireland's oldest pub, established in 1189, and James Joyce was a regular at Davy Byrnes. In stark contrast are the Docklands. In Dublin's newly redeveloped harbor district, futuristic glass buildings dominate the skyline – tech giants like Facebook and Airbnb have their European headquarters here. The harbor is also home to a replica of the famous tall ship the Jeanie Johnston – commemorating the many Irish families who fled the famine in the mid 19th century and set sail for North America. Anyone interested in learning more should definitely visit The Irish Emigration Museum. As a fan of theater and music, I also always send friends to The Gaiety Theatre – and, of course, to the Bord Gáis Energy Theatre by architect Daniel Libeskind – a spectacular glass building that guarantees an entertaining night out with musicals, comedians, and more. About As head chef at the fine dining restaurant Glas, Malte Half is known for his imaginative vegetarian and vegan creations, which feature ingredients like wild Irish herbs and seaweed.
