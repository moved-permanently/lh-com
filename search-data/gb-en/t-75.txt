From SoHo to Brooklyn – Experience the Highlights of Fashion Week.","New York Fashion Week is irresistible to fashion lovers like Lufthansa flight attendant Bourtsin Kioutsouk. For a few days, the city is transformed into a giant runway, where international designers showcase the coolest street styles and the latest trends. Bourtsin tells us how she is inspired by New York Fashion Week – and we asked the Lufthansa community for tips on what travelers shouldn't miss out on 1. Keep an Eye Out for Inspiring Street Styles As a fashion lover, New York Fashion Week is an absolute highlight for me. But tickets to the shows are in high demand and hard to come by. I often just wander around the city looking for amazing street styles – spontaneity rules! Street fashion is just as inspiring as what's shown on the runways during this time. Of course, fashion enthusiasts tend to congregate at known places like the Starrett-Lehigh Building in Chelsea. That's where I go to see how people from all over the world express themselves and their style. 2. Shop on Fashion Avenue, in Midtown, and in SoHo Another must-do in New York is an extensive shopping tour on Seventh Avenue in the Garment District, where it is also known as Fashion Avenue. It’s a paradise for fashion lovers like me. Alongside luxury boutiques, you’ll find unique concept stores. I also recommend visiting the Dover Street Market on Lexington Avenue – it’s about a 20-minute walk from Grand Central Station. This is where I hunt for standout pieces that no one else back home will be wearing. Also worth your time is taking a trip to SoHo to browse the many small shops. 3. Dine in Style at Polo Bar or Balthazar I love exploring New York on foot, so I need to take a break every now and then to rest my feet. Ralph Lauren's Polo Bar and Balthazar in SoHo are always on my list. These restaurants are known not only for their fantastic food, but also for the many fashion personalities who gather there, especially during New York Fashion Week. Will I see a familiar face this time? 4. Explore Iconic Locations The Meatpacking District, with its stylish bars and galleries, is at the top of my list during New York Fashion Week. You can often see fashion shoots in action on the streets and along the High Line – a repurposed ex-rail line that is a green oasis in the city. The creative vibe and breathtaking atmosphere provide the perfect backdrop for beautifully staged photos. I love walking through this neighborhood and soaking up its infectious energy. Discover Emerging Designers Fashion Week is not only when established designers showcase their work, it is also an opportunity for debutants to present their fresh, inspiring creations. It's worth doing a little research ahead of time to see who is making their debut at the New York shows. Some may already have their own boutiques or even a pop-up store. 6. Take a Break A little time away from the fashion frenzy can be refreshing. A visit to Chinatown or Chelsea Market is a welcome change of pace. Seeing the city from above is always an impressive experience, whether from the classic vantage point of Rockefeller Center, with its breathtaking view of the Empire State Building, or from Summit One Vanderbilt, the tallest skyscraper in Midtown. A visit to the Museum of Modern Art or the Metropolitan Museum of Art is also a must. For me, art is just as inspiring as fashion. 7. Visit Legendary Stylist Patricia Field’s Store When the protagonists are impeccably styled in movies or TV shows, Patricia Field is usually responsible. She's best known for her work on “Sex and the City,” but she also created iconic looks for “The Devil Wears Prada” and “Emily in Paris”. That's why a visit to Patricia Field's ArtFashion Gallery at 211 East Broadway on the Lower East Side is a must for me. The shop is a fusion of art gallery and boutique – a true fashion lover's paradise. 8. Explore New York's Streetwear Labels New York and streetwear is a match made in heaven. No other city has shaped streetwear culture like it. In the early 1980s, a movement emerged that fused fashion, music, and art – heavily influenced by the hip-hop, graffiti, and skate scenes. This culture gave rise to iconic brands like Supreme, Kith, and Aimé Leon Dore. I love exploring the Lower East Side, where younger labels like Laams and Extra Butter have their stores. 9. Experience Fashion in Brooklyn During Fashion Week, Brooklyn becomes a hotspot for independent designers and artists, with many pop-up shops and events in warehouses and galleries. I recommend taking the ferry from the Financial District. Just a few minutes' walk from the Dumbo ferry terminal is the Brooklyn Ice Cream Factory, where I grab a scoop to go before exploring the neighborhood. The atmosphere here is a refreshing contrast to the hustle and bustle of Manhattan. My journey continues to Williamsburg and Bushwick, where a thriving creative scene brings fashion and art to life. 10. ["Artist Stefan Marx shares his best travel hacks and how he turns every journey into a special experience.","As an artist, Stefan Marx travels all over the world to find inspiration, to draw – and to hit the reset button along the way. He shares his best travel hacks with Lufthansa Insights Stefan Marx is world-famous for his typographic art, and he is extremely interested in aviation. A perfect match with Lufthansa! He has already designed a Lufthansa lounge in Miami Beach for Art Basel and has created images inspired by air travel. Most recently, he has designed loungewear for Lufthansa First Class guests that bears the slogan IT'S YESTERDAY SOMEWHERE ELSE, which is a meditation on the feeling and perception of time when traveling. Here, he reveals his personal tricks and tips for relaxed flying. 1. Getting Creative at the Airport I try to avoid very early morning flights. Since I like spending time at the airport and drawing, I get there much earlier than necessary anyway. I know some terminals pretty well by now and usually sit in my favorite spots, preferably with a view of the tarmac, and make drawings. There are good places to linger in almost all airports. If it's my first time at an airport, I start by exploring it in detail. And it’s very important to recognize that creativity needs space, which is why I always work through as much of my to-do list as possible before long-haul flights. That way, I have a clear head and don't have to think about what still needs to be done during the flight. 2. Travel Light, but Have the Essentials with You I'm one of those people who also checks in smaller pieces of luggage, because I like experiencing the area after security as carefree as possible. I usually have a few snacks with me, fruit or something salty. Otherwise, I look forward to the airline's food. What belongs in my personal amenity kit? A few personal items for my well-being, as well as drawing supplies: a pad of paper, my favorite pens, and a small notebook that I always carry with me anyway. On longer flights, I also carry noise-canceling headphones, an eye mask, and a change of clothes. Stefan Marx always has his sketchpad with him on the plane. (©Lufthansa; header image: © Timothy Schaumburg) 3. The Right Clothes for Every Situation When it comes to clothing for traveling by plane, comfort is paramount. On night flights, I like to wear a hoodie, loose T-shirts, and comfortable pants. When it's time to sleep, I put on the pajamas provided by the airline. I'm already looking forward to my first flight in the loungewear I designed for Lufthansa First Class, which features the slogan IT'S YESTERDAY SOMEWHERE ELSE. It's super comfortable and just feels great. 4. Just Let the Jet Lag Happen Most travelers try to get rid of jet lag as quickly as possible. To be honest, I love jet lag. If I have the time, I just give in to it: sleep when I'm tired and go out when I'm awake. Not being able to fall asleep at three in the morning in Japan is great! Then, I just wander the streets and see what's going on. When I get home, I do the same; the jet lag goes away on its own – faster than I'd like ...
