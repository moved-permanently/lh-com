How do you guarantee this, and why is this certification so important for you and for Lufthansa? Airlines such as Lufthansa expect the products they have on board to be halal-compliant so that they do not have to run several kitchen lines in parallel. For us, for example, this means that when we process salmon from aquaculture, we have to prove that the fish feed is free of pork proteins. The job comes with a specific set of requirements – from strong German and English skills and a height between 1.55 and 1.95 meters (between 5'1\" and 6'5\") to solid general knowledge of the world. Just as important are the soft skills: a high degree of flexibility, openness and ability to adapt to constantly changing crews, people, and situations – and above all, perseverance. Crew members can sometimes be awake for up to 30 hours at a time, as working days can be long and demanding. Young and optimistic, I was confident that my experience working in hospitality during my school years had prepared me to meet those requirements. Then the application process began: an online application, an English test, and finally an invitation to the assessment center in Frankfurt. The most exciting part was the psychological evaluation, designed to determine whether candidates were suited to the responsibility of carrying out duties in the cabin. This was followed by the medical assessment and a background check conducted by the aviation security authorities. I received a job offer, and a few months later I began my training at Lufthansa Aviation Training in Schwaig, Bavaria. Unlike many other professional training programs, the theoretical portion of flight attendant training is considerably shorter and more intensive, lasting just three months. Divided into two sections, the first part focused entirely on onboard service – something that came naturally to me. Marie Hecker has been a Lufthansa flight attendant for 14 years (© Yves Bachmann/Lufthansa) Even on long workdays, friendly and attentive service is always part of the job (© Yves Bachmann/Lufthansa) Even before her training, Hecker gained valuable experience for onboard service while working in the restaurant industry (© Felix Wittich/Lufthansa) The second part was entirely new to me – and far more demanding. All of this took place around 15 years ago, and much has changed since then. Today, far more steps in the process are conducted digitally: parts of the training are completed online in advance, and the traditional assessment center has been replaced by more modern formats, such as casting sessions. On the Lufthansa Super Star, they had access to chess sets and small typewriters. A 1959 passenger brochure states: “The hours on board fly by, and you should use this time to relax. There are chess sets and other board games for long-haul flights, as well as cards, puzzle books, and current newspapers and magazines. The flight attendant will be happy to bring them to you.” During the 1950s and 1960s, the painter and graphic designer created numerous iconic travel posters for Lufthansa, sparking a longing for faraway destinations such as Bangkok, New York, and Tokyo. His mid-century modern artworks won numerous awards and made him famous. As early as 1968, Lufthansa proved itself a pioneer in providing care above the clouds. That is when the “Rotkäppchen-Service” (“Little Red Riding Hood Service”) was launched, a support program for children traveling alone, young families, and passengers with special needs. The striking red headwear worn by staff members earned them their fairy-tale nickname. Today, the identifying mark is more modern yet just as memorable: a red button bearing the words “Special Service.” Since the 1930s, Lufthansa has provided scarves to protect against the cold in the cabin. In 1970, the scarf became a trademark when designer Werner Machnik outfitted flight attendants with blue and yellow scarves to match their mini-style, blue uniforms with gold-plated buttons – a nod to the Swinging Sixties. Tableware and cutlery are an airline’s calling card, and Lufthansa recognized this early on. The first famous designer to create in-flight tableware for Lufthansa was Bauhaus student Wilhelm Wagenfeld in 1955. He was a pioneer of modern industrial design. In the 1970s, Hans Theo Baumann continued this clean design language. With his tableware, he set new standards for in-flight dining. The fitness trend became a mass phenomenon in the 1980s, and was also embraced by Lufthansa. The airline released a cassette on the topic. Today, Lufthansa offers special exercise videos in its in-flight program on long-haul flights. These videos feature exercises that can be done in your seat and are designed to promote blood circulation and relieve tension.
