["Discover the best things to do in Riyadh at a glance: find out what to eat, which sights to see, and which activities to experience.","Here are our three tips for an inspiring stay in Riyadh. At first glance, the Saudi Arabian capital appears to be a young metropolis of glass, asphalt, and wide boulevards, but beneath its modern surface lies a history stretching back centuries. Historic sites like At-Turaif in Diriyah, modern cultural and leisure destinations such as Boulevard City, and its location on a desert plateau create an urban atmosphere that captivates visitors less with individual sights and more with its fascinating contrasts Anyone wishing to explore Riyadh’s culinary scene should sample the traditional cuisine of the Najd region, which is where Riyadh is located. At the Najd Village restaurant, classic dishes are served in a deliberately traditional setting: with low seating areas, carpets, and a cozy atmosphere where sharing a meal takes center stage. The menu features typical regional dishes in which spices and slow-cooked ingredients are the stars of the show. (© Getty Images; header image: © Shutterstock) More Culinary Delights: Kabsa: Kabsa is one of Saudi Arabia’s most famous dishes. In Riyadh, it appears in countless variations and is a good example of just how different a classic dish can taste. Rice and meat are served on large platters – often chicken, but also lamb, beef, or occasionally camel. Kabsa gets its characteristic flavor from an aromatic spice blend made from ingredients such as cilantro, cumin, pepper, cardamom, saffron, cinnamon, and cloves. The sauce is made from tomatoes and sautéed onions. Jareesh: Jareesh is a traditional dish from the Najd region and is made from coarsely ground wheat that is cooked until it reaches a thick, almost porridge-like consistency. Depending on the region and family, Jareesh is prepared with lamb or chicken, often enhanced with yogurt or milk, and served with ghee and roasted onions. This warm, hearty comfort food is also served in Saudi Arabia on special occasions and even for breakfast. Sabeeb: Sabeeb are among the classic desserts of the Najd region. They resemble small, thick pancakes and are made from a batter of flour, water or milk, and yeast. Baked in a pan until golden brown, Sabeeb are usually served with honey, date syrup, or butter. In Saudi Arabia, they are traditionally eaten with coffee or tea. At-Turaif in Diriyah is one of the most important historical sites in the Riyadh area and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2010. The mud-brick buildings in this neighborhood were constructed in the 15th century and showcase the typical architectural style of Central Arabia, featuring courtyards, massive walls, and a clear, geometric design language. In the 18th and early 19th centuries, At-Turaif was the political center of the Saudi dynasty. Today, the origins of modern Saudi Arabia can be vividly traced among the palace ruins and restored buildings. (© Getty Images) Also Worth Seeing: National Museum of Saudi Arabia: The National Museum of Saudi Arabia is one of Riyadh’s most important cultural sites and offers a comprehensive insight into the history of the Arabian Peninsula. Around 4,400 exhibits are presented in several thematic exhibition halls: from archaeological finds and fossils to historical weapons, furniture, and textiles, as well as other everyday objects. The exhibitions guide visitors through various eras of the region and show how culture, religion, and society have developed on the Arabian Peninsula. As one of the largest museums in the region, it is designed so that visitors can easily spend several hours here. Al Masmak Fort: Located in the heart of the historic ad-Dirah district, Al Masmak is one of Riyadh’s key historical sites. The mud-brick fortress was built in the 19th century and is closely linked to the city’s history: in 1902, King Abdulaziz conquered the fortress – a pivotal event on the path to the later unification of Saudi Arabia. Today, Al Masmak houses a museum that tells the story of the emergence of the modern Saudi state through photos, maps, weapons, and other historical objects. Sky Bridge at the Kingdom Centre: At around 300 meters (985 feet) tall, the Kingdom Centre is one of Riyadh’s most striking buildings, its unusual shape – reminiscent of a bottle opener – defining the city’s skyline. At the very top, the more than 65-meter-long (213 feet) Sky Bridge connects the two spires of the tower. From the spectacular observation deck, a sweeping view opens up over Riyadh’s skyscrapers, boulevards, and the city’s seemingly endless expanse. Especially in the evening, when Riyadh slowly begins to glow, a walk across the bridge offers one of the most impressive views of the Saudi capital. About 100 kilometers (62 miles) from Riyadh, the Tuwaik mountain range ends at a viewpoint that truly lives up to its name: Edge of the World (Jebel Fihrayn). Here, the cliffs drop abruptly into a vast plain, and at twilight, the landscape looks almost like an artistically drawn graphic – rocks, sky, horizon. The excursion is a true hiking adventure and, at the same time, a tranquil experience of vastness, against which Riyadh suddenly seems very small. (© Shutterstock) More Experiences: Desert safari just outside the city: The landscape outside Riyadh, with its dunes, rock formations, and vast plains, offers the ideal setting for a desert safari. Guided tours usually take you through the sandy landscape around the city in an off-road vehicle, often combined with short stops for photography, sandboarding, or an evening in a traditional Bedouin setting. Sunset excursions are particularly atmospheric, when the dunes glow in the warm light. Boulevard City: Boulevard City – often simply called “The Boulevard” – is a large, modern entertainment and leisure district in the Hittin neighborhood. The approximately 90-hectare (220-acre) area is now considered one of the city’s main entertainment hubs. Various themed zones feature restaurants, cafés, shops, outdoor cinemas, event stages, gaming areas, and dancing fountains. Concerts, festivals, and sporting events are also held here regularly. As the central stage of Riyadh Season, Boulevard City now serves as a hub for much of Riyadh’s nightlife and leisure activities. King Abdullah Park: King Abdullah Park in the Al Malaz district is one of the most beautiful places for anyone who wants to experience Riyadh beyond the boulevards, malls, and skyscrapers. The site, formerly known as Al-Malaz Square, now features expansive lawns, gardens, and wide paths. It’s a place for walks, picnics, and a break in the greenery. In the evenings, the large, illuminated fountain draws many visitors: the water dances to music and light, giving the park a festival-like atmosphere. Ready to discover Riyadh? ["Discover the best things to do in St. Louis at a glance, with tips on food, sights, and experiences for an inspiring stay.","St. Louis still bears the nickname \"Gateway to the West,\" a reference to the historical role of the city as a starting point for the expansion of the USA into the areas west of the Mississippi. The region is also known for its industrial history, blues, baseball, and special regional cuisine. It is said to have been created in the 1930s thanks to a mistake made by a German-American bakery in the city. It is characterized to this day by the soft center under the slightly caramelized surface. St. Louis-style pizza: This rectangular pizza has a particularly thin base and is traditionally topped with Provel, a regional cheese mixture of provolone, cheddar, and Emmental. The cheese is much creamier than mozzarella when it melts and gives the pizza its typical, slightly smoky taste, which is rather controversial outside of St. Louis. Frozen custard at Ted Drewes: The ice cream parlor on the historic Route 66 has been one of the city's best-known locations for decades. Frozen custard is served there – a particularly dense and creamy form of soft ice cream mixed with ingredients such as biscuits, chocolate, or fruit. The best-known variants are so firm that the cups are traditionally turned upside down briefly before they are handed over the counter. The silver steel arch on the banks of the Mississippi is visible from afar, a testimony to the fact that St. Louis is still considered the \"Gateway to the West\" today. The 192-meter-high (630 feet) Gateway Arch was built in the 1960s according to a design by architect Eero Saarinen and commemorates the historic westward expansion of the United States. Directly below are the associated museum and the old areas where St. Louis was originally founded. (© Stocksy) Also Worth Seeing: Forest Park: The city park hosted the 1904 World's Fair and is even larger than Central Park in New York. Lakes, wide avenues, and historic buildings invite you to visit. Cathedral Basilica of St. Louis: This church has one of the largest mosaic collections in the world outside of Russia. Over 40 million individual mosaic tiles cover the walls and domes of the building. City Museum: What used to be a shoe factory has recently been converted into a world of tunnels, caves, metal slides, and recycled industrial elements. Instead of classic exhibitions, the path here takes guests through narrow corridors, former factory structures, and up to the roof, where an old school bus protrudes over the edge of the building and amazes visitors. The St. Louis Cardinals are one of the most successful and traditional teams in the Baseball League and have won the championship eleven times so far – only the New York Yankees have managed more. Around Busch Stadium, downtown turns almost completely red on game days, when fans in their jerseys fill the bars and streets hours before the first pitch. Many followers travel from all over the Midwest, because the \"Cardinal Nation\" extends far beyond Missouri. It's an experience that makes it clear how important baseball is in this region of the USA to this day. (© Shutterstock) More Experiences: Walk through Soulard: Soulard is one of the oldest neighborhoods in St. Louis, dating back to 18th-century French settlers. Cobblestones, brick houses, and the Soulard Farmers Market are reminders of the city's early immigration history. Now, blues bars, small pubs, and music venues open their doors here in the evenings. Brewery tour at Anheuser-Busch: Anheuser-Busch beer has been brewed in St. Louis since 1852. The complex south of Soulard comprises dozens of historic brick buildings including towers, vaults, and old brewhouses and almost looks like a district in its own right. During the brewery tour, you can take a look at cellars, historic breweries, and the famous Budweiser Clydesdales. The horses have been a symbol of the brand since the 1930s. Listen to music in the Delmar Loop in the evening: Over the decades, the Delmar Loop developed into one of St. Louis' most important nightlife districts. Small clubs, concert venues, and record stores keep the city's blues and live music scene alive to this day. By the way: Chuck Berry performed here regularly for many years.
