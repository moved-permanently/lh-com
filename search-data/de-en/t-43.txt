["Red Hong Yi shares her creative neighborhood Kampung Attap in Kuala Lumpur, from art and cafés to galleries, restaurants, and local hot spots.","Red Hong Yi is one of Malaysia’s best-known artists and creates art from everyday objects – most recently, buttons. In the video, she shows us where she likes to shop for them and takes us to some of her favorite restaurants, cafés, and galleries in Kampung Attap, Kuala Lumpur’s creative hub Concrete, glass, and steel were once Red Hong Yi’s materials of choice. Today, she works with buttons, coins, eggshells, chopsticks, tea bags, soil, salt, socks, and matchsticks. Many of her works are made from vast quantities of small objects or everyday materials, earning Red the nickname “the artist who paints without a paintbrush.” Born in Malaysia, Red originally studied architecture in Melbourne. After completing her master’s degree, she joined Australian architecture firm Hassell in Shanghai, designing skyscrapers across Asia for the region’s then-booming construction industry. “When I designed a high-rise, I never knew whether it would actually get built,” Red says. “And even if it did, it would take at least six or seven years. I kept thinking, wow, I don’t even know if I’ll still be at the same company by then – or even in the same country.” So she began creating smaller art projects instead. “Something I could finish in a month,” Red says. She posted images of her work on social media and soon began receiving requests for commissioned pieces. “Some of the fees were pretty attractive,” she says. “So I decided to take the leap and become a full-time artist.” Her boss at the time made the decision easier. “He encouraged me and assured me that I could come back to the office if it didn’t work out. I had nothing to lose.” Today, Red lives and works in Kuala Lumpur – far from the world’s major international art capitals, but very much by choice. Malaysia, she says, is and always will be home. Her studio is in the central neighborhood of Kampung Attap, an area that has turned into a creative hub over the years – and one that the artist shows us around in the video. They have already been shown at the VOLTA Art Fair in Basel and the Future Fair in New York, among others. Info: katrinasanchez.com Ready to discover the world?
