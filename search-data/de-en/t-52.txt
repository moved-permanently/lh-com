["Get ready for Venice, Italy: Enjoy a relaxed trip with info on the vaporetto, coperto, opening hours, and more.","Amid quiet canals, winding alleys, and magnificent palaces, Venice exudes a unique charm that can only be fully appreciated on foot or by vaporetto (water bus); cars are prohibited in the historic city center. Markets, cafés, and hidden squares invite you to explore, while the waterways connect the city in a truly unique way. Here you’ll find all the information you need for a relaxing stay Venice Marco Polo Airport (VCE) is located about 13 kilometers (8 miles) from the city center. The most affordable option is the bus (ATVO or ACTV) for about €10; the ride takes approximately 20 to 30 minutes to the Venezia stop at Piazzale Roma. Alternatively, water buses (Alilaguna) run directly across the lagoon for about €18. Water taxis are significantly more expensive, but offer direct access to many hotels. Venice is best explored on foot and by water. Within the historic center, there are no cars or public buses, and even bicycles are prohibited. Most routes wind through narrow alleyways, across bridges, and around charming squares. For longer journeys, crossings of the Grand Canal, or trips to islands such as Murano, Burano, Giudecca, and Lido, the ACTV vaporetto water buses serve as the city's primary form of public transportation. A single ticket is valid for 75 minutes and costs about €10, while day passes and multi-day travel cards (starting at around €25) are generally the more economical option for visitors planning multiple trips throughout the day. Gondolas, by contrast, are considered more of a tourist experience than a mode of transportation. Tipping is not mandatory in Venice, but it is appreciated for good service. In many restaurants, the bill already includes a “coperto” (cover charge) of usually €2 to €5 per person; if a “servizio” is also listed, the service charge is already included and an additional tip is not required. The tip is rarely mentioned out loud when paying; it is more common to discreetly leave coins or small bills on the table or on the bill holder. Shops usually open between 9:30 a.m. and 7:30 p.m., often with a break for lunch. Supermarkets are open all day, some until 9:00 p.m. Restaurants rarely start their evening service before 7:00 p.m., with peak hours beginning at 8:00 p.m. Although Sundays are quieter, tourist areas remain very busy. Picnics in historic squares such as St. Mark’s Square are not permitted. In addition, feeding the pigeons is banned and may result in fines. Swimming in the canals is also strictly prohibited. Venice consists of over 100 small islands connected by more than 400 bridges. The city was built on millions of wooden piles anchored deep into the lagoon bed. Despite the frequent flooding in Venice (“Acqua alta”), this construction method has remained impressively stable over the centuries. Ready to discover Venice? The best transport system in Zagreb is its extensive tram network. The blue trams have been a feature of the cityscape for over a hundred years and connect almost all the central districts – from the Upper Town to Novi Zagreb.
