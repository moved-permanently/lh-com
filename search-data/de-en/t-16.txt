On one side is the riverbank, on the other the beautiful old buildings, plus small cafés and that uniquely Berlin mix of everyday life and the city's cultural scene. When the twice-weekly market is on, things are naturally especially lively. In few other cities is it as evident just how profoundly political upheavals have impacted people’s lives as it is in Berlin. That’s exactly why, for me, the Berlin Wall Memorial on Bernauer Straße is an absolute must-see on this day trip. It conveys very powerfully what really happened in Berlin. I think it’s much more tangible than at other well-known Berlin Wall sites because here you don’t just look at history – you really feel it. I like that so much of it is outdoors. You walk along the former border strip, look at photos, read texts, and see remnants of the wall, and get a very clear sense of just how brutally this city was once divided. For me, this place is an essential part of Berlin because it brings the memory of one of the darkest chapters in German and European history to the heart of the city. That’s exactly what I appreciate about Berlin: the city isn’t just vibrant and full of contrasts – it also takes responsibility for its past. In my opinion, this form of remembrance is an essential part of any day in Berlin. For me personally, the Tränenpalast on Friedrichstraße is a very moving and dramatic place where you immediately understand how divided the city was in the past. ["Discover Houston in one day: purser Tim Baur explains how to plan the perfect day trip to the Texan metropolis.","Purser Tim Baur takes us on a day trip to Houston, where he reveals why you absolutely have to try the fajitas – and why the city defies all the usual Texas stereotypes Because of the time difference, I usually wake up early in Houston and immediately feel the urge to get outside. This really pays off in spring and summer, as it often gets very hot later in the day. Houston sits on the Gulf of Mexico, which gives it a subtropical climate with high humidity. As a result, the city is incredibly green and lush. I especially love Hermann Park in downtown Houston, where I like to stroll in the morning and watch the city wake up. It feels like the whole city is out and about, and it’s easy to strike up conversations with locals. A green oasis: in Hermann Park, in the heart of Houston, tall trees provide shade on hot summer days (© Mauritius/Alamy; header image © Getty Images) The Sunday Press Café is set on the edge of the lake in Hermann Park. I like to start my day there with a coffee or a light breakfast and then continue my walk through the park’s Japanese Garden – or simply look up at the majestic old oak trees that shade the paths. Another highlight is the concerts, dance, and theater performances at the Miller Outdoor Theatre, which take place regularly in the park from spring through fall. The Houston Zoo is right in the heart of Hermann Park. It may not be record-breaking in size, but it features a thoughtful layout – and it’s perfect for a quick visit. Despite its size, it boasts impressive diversity, and you suddenly find yourself standing in front of okapis, watching chimpanzees, or observing prehistoric-looking tortoises from the Galápagos Islands. The city’s Greek community has also settled here – and the restaurant scene is shaped by influences from all over the world. In Montrose, you absolutely have to try Greek food. I particularly enjoyed my meal at Niko Niko’s, a restaurant that regularly ranks among the best Greek spots in the city. My tip: don’t miss the Feta Fries – French fries generously topped with crumbled feta and oregano. Retrospect Coffee Bar, located in a former gas station from the 1920s, is also well worth a stop. Wherever I go in Houston, people are warm, open, and friendly – which I really appreciate. I also love that they bring unusual, slightly crazy ideas to life. The Beer Can House in the Rice Military neighborhood is a perfect example of this. The entire house is decorated from top to bottom with more than 50,000 beer cans and includes a gallery featuring works by local artists. It’s a must-see. Montrose is also great for shopping. There are countless secondhand shops and small boutiques where you can find one-of-a-kind souvenirs – for example, at the Montrose Collective, a small shopping center filled with independent retailers, or at vintage stores like The Fashion Time Machine and Out Of The Closet. Dinner at the Tex-Mex restaurant The Original Ninfa’s, which opened in 1973 on Navigation Boulevard, is an absolute must-visit. No:1 The Galata Tower offers a wonderful view of the city, but you have to wait a long time in the queue. I therefore prefer to go to the other side of Galata Bridge. There I treat myself to a delicious lunch in the 360-Panorama-Restaurant and enjoy the breathtaking view of the Blue Mosque and Hagia Sophia. Of course, you should also take a closer look at the two places of worship – a must for anyone visiting Istanbul for the first time. From the restaurant, it’s just a few steps to Istanbul’s famous Grand Bazaar. Here I let myself drift between thousands of stalls and stores. Everything imaginable is on offer: handmade carpets, mosaic lamps, olive oil soap, ceramics, and bath towels. The latter is very useful if you want to visit a hammam, a Turkish bath, afterward. Very close to the bazaar is the historic Hürrem Sultan Hammam, which dates back to 1556. Here I escape the hustle and bustle of the city and immerse myself in the soothing atmosphere of the traditional bath. Surrounded by fine wood and marble, I immediately relax with a foam massage. At the end, the staff there even wash and comb your hair – a moment that makes me feel like a princess from the Ottoman era. The best time for a Bosporus cruise is after sunset. When the lights of the city are reflected in the water at night, Istanbul is a dream. The excursion boats depart from Ortaköy harbor. If you still have energy left, you can end the day in one of Ortaköy’s many bars and restaurants. For example in The House Café, where DJs play on the terrace at the weekend – and you can enjoy the view of the city’s sea of lights.
