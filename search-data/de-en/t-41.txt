On Commercial Drive, which stretches for three and a half kilometers from the sea to the south of Vancouver, it’s the other way around. Vintage boutiques, tattoo shops and family-run restaurants such as my favorite Italian place, Marcello, line the street. Lots of little sights. There used to be a branch of a famous US coffee shop chain on \"the Drive\", as the street is called by locals, but it had to close quickly due to the huge competition from owner-run cafés. There are 19 of them now. I love drinking a cappuccino at Café Calabria, which has been in business since 1976 and is run by an Italian family. I treat myself to Sicilian pastries and, as so often on the Drive, I feel like I’m in Italy. Eight blocks of the neighborhood are known as Little Italy. Zebra crossings are painted green, white and red here and you are surrounded by a babbel of Italian voices in the shops. However, many other nations are represented, too. In the colorful Havana Bar I feel like I’m in Cuba, and when dining in Kishimoto, a wonderful sushi restaurant, I feel like I’m in Japan.
