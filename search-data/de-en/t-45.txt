Get your bearings with these tips for your visit to the “white city,” so called because of the light-colored buildings on the hills around this metropolis of the Middle East Queen Alia International Airport lies about 35 kilometers (22 miles) south of central Amman, and it takes 35 to 60 minutes to get to the city depending on traffic. The Sariyah Airport Express Bus is the most affordable option, running a regular schedule to the Tabarbour bus station in the city, with several stops on the way. It costs around 3–4 Jordanian dinar (JOD). Yellow airport taxis operate around the clock, with fixed fares to destinations in the city. Expect to pay about 15 to 25 JOD in cash to get to most central districts. Ride‑hailing services like Uber and Careem are generally available and may be cheaper, but pickups may be restricted to specific zones in the airport. Public transport within Amman centers mainly on buses and minibuses. A ride is usually inexpensive (under 1 JOD), but routes and timetables may be confusing for visitors. Taxis are widely available and are metered; a typical short ride within the city may cost 2–4 JOD, while cross‑town trips reach around 5–7 JOD. Ride‑hailing services provide clear pricing and the ability to pay by card or in‑app. The city is hilly and spread out, with heavy traffic on main roads. Walking is an option for exploring specific neighborhoods like Downtown and the hilly districts of Jabal Al‑Weibdeh and Jabal Amman, but getting between them might be a challenge. Cycle routes are limited. The Jordanian dinar (JOD) is subdivided into 100 piastres or 1,000 fils. Banknotes in common usage range from 1 to 50 JOD. The economy is still cash‑oriented in many everyday situations, especially in small shops, markets, and older cafés. International cards are widely accepted in larger hotels, modern restaurants, malls, and international chains, and contactless payments and digital wallets such as Apple Pay and Google Pay are increasingly welcome in newer establishments. ATMs are widespread in commercial areas. It is advisable to carry a mix of cash in small denominations and at least one major credit card. Keep in mind that Friday is the day of rest, with the week set up accordingly. Shops in Amman usually open from around 10 a.m. until 8:30 p.m., with malls and larger retail centers sometimes staying open later, especially on weekends. Many shops close around midday between 1:00 and 1:30 p.m. During Ramadan, many businesses open later and stay open well into the evening, with a quiet period around sunset for iftar, or the breaking of the fast.
