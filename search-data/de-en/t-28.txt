["Discover the best things to do in Denver: from bison and green chili to Red Rocks and outdoor adventures in the Rocky Mountains.","Here are our three tips for an inspiring stay in Denver, the capital of Colorado at the foot of the Rocky Mountains. Named the Mile High City because of its location at an altitude of 1,609 meters (5,280 feet), the metropolis combines creative cuisine, striking architecture, and immediate proximity to nature in a way that feels urban while at the same time showcasing a deep connection to the outdoors. It's a destination with something for everyone In Colorado, bison is almost as common a sight on the menu as the snow on the peaks of the Rocky Mountains. This cloven-hoofed animal, symbolic of the American West, was long considered virtually extinct in the region and was reintroduced in the 20th century through conservation programs. Since the herds have recovered, bison meat has featured more often in Western cuisine. In the Lincoln Park neighborhood, for example at the Buckhorn Exchange – the city’s oldest restaurant – it is served in the traditional style: rich in flavor yet surprisingly tender in texture. This dish defines Denver in terms of both cuisine and history. (© Mauritius; header image © Getty Images) More Culinary Delights: Rocky Mountain oysters: The name might suggest that these are oysters. But think again! Behind the name lies a rustic specialty of breaded and deep-fried bull testicles, which has been a staple of ranch cuisine in Colorado since the 19th century and is served with a hot sauce. Green chili: You could almost say that green chili is to Denver what beer is to Bavaria. This isn’t a dip, but a spicy, usually creamy sauce or a stew-like chili made from green chilies, traditionally often with pork, but now also available in vegetarian versions. Depending on the recipe, it is cooked with or without tomatoes. Generously poured over burritos or breakfast plates, green chili clearly reflects the influence of the American Southwest. Specialty coffee from RiNo: Anyone on the hunt for “liquid gold” will find a thriving scene of independent coffee spots in the River North Art District. Featuring murals, old warehouses, and creative studios, the neighborhood is characterized not only by breweries but also by roasteries and cafés. For this reason, RiNo has long been more than just a brewery district when it comes to cuisine. About 25 kilometers (15.5 miles) west of Denver lies the Red Rocks Amphitheatre, an open-air theater that was not built from scratch but set into a natural rock formation of red sandstone. The acoustics are considered exceptional, yet even without a concert, the venue is impressive. The best time to visit is in the morning: while on days without events the site is open from early morning until evening, on event days it is often only open until around 2 p.m. Many come solely for the architecture, the rock formations, and the sweeping view across the Great Plains to the Denver skyline. (© Getty Images) Also Worth Seeing: Denver Art Museum: The spectacular Hamilton Building was designed by renowned architect Daniel Libeskind and, with its façade of angular titanium and glass panels, intentionally echoes the silhouette of the Rocky Mountains. The museum houses one of the biggest collections of Indigenous art in North America. Union Station: For decades, the station was regarded as the main transport hub in the western United States. Even today, the large iconic Travel by Train signage on the exterior façade bears witness to the golden age of rail travel. Union Station has now become a hybrid space, combining a railway station with a public living room. Colorado State Capitol: The government building of the state of Colorado bears a strong resemblance to the US Capitol in Washington, DC. A notable difference, however, is the dome, which has been lavishly covered in real gold leaf, a reference to the 19th-century gold rush. On a clear day, the Colorado State Capitol offers sweeping views all the way to the Rocky Mountains. About an hour and a half northwest of Denver, Rocky Mountain National Park marks the start of a whole new world. Those who set off early can witness the light slowly spreading across the peaks, alpine tundra, and clear mountain lakes. The crisp mountain air, the sweeping panorama, and the morning silence highlight just how close the wilderness is to the city, showcasing northern Colorado from yet another breathtaking angle. (© Getty Images) More Experiences: Cycle tour on the Cherry Creek Trail: This cycle path, just under 77 kilometers (48 miles) long, not only connects Downtown Denver with Cherry Creek State Park but also extends much further into the countryside to the southeast. Large sections of the route are car-free, passing through a wide variety of city areas and landscapes, from urban stretches to more open, tranquil sections. It is this length and diversity that make the trail a fine example of Denver’s cycle-friendly side. Baseball match at Coors Field: Baseball fans take note: the Colorado Rockies’ home ground is situated at a particularly high altitude, which means that due to the thinner air, batted balls usually fly further. This makes the matches particularly exciting for spectators. On a clear day, the upper tiers even offer views of the Rocky Mountains. The worlds of Meow Wolf: Anyone in Denver looking for an experience beyond traditional museums will find it in this walk-through art world. Within the multistory complex, four interconnected universes unfold, featuring hidden passageways, light and sound installations, and a story that can be discovered bit by bit. Ready to discover Denver, the Mile High City? Gateway to the Rocky Mountains, with authentic Western cuisine and the Red Rocks Amphitheatre – book your flight to the capital of Colorado. ["Discover the best things to do in Chennai at a glance, with tips on food, sights, and experiences for an inspiring stay.","For centuries, Chennai was one of the major port towns on the southeastern Coromandel coast and it is still considered the cultural center of South India today. Temple bells, jasmine blossoms at market stalls, and the honking of auto rickshaws are just as much a part of everyday life there as classical South Indian music and cricket on the beach. Our three tips showcase the culinary, sightseeing, and historical side to Chennai In Chennai, the day often starts with idli, dosa, or pongal – served on a banana leaf and accompanied by sambar and various chutneys. Idlis are softly steamed rice cakes made from fermented rice and lentil dough, while dosas are large, thinly fried crêpes with crispy edges. Visually, pongal is somewhat reminiscent of a hearty risotto made of rice and lentils, seasoned with black pepper, cumin, and curry leaves. Restaurants such as Murugan Idli Shop or Saravana Bhavan have been serving these classics almost unchanged for decades. (© Getty Images; header image © Shutterstock) More Culinary Delights: Coffee: South Indian coffee is traditionally served in small stainless steel cups and poured back and forth between two vessels several times to create foam and lower the temperature. The slow brewing through metal filters provides a strong, slightly caramel taste. Chettinad cuisine: The cuisine of the Chettinad region, south of Chennai, is considered one of the spiciest in India. Black pepper, star anise, fennel seeds, and curry leaves accompany many meat and fish dishes. Street food on Marina Beach: Along the beach promenade, small stalls sell fried fish, bajji (dumplings), and sundal – a warm snack made from chickpeas, coconut, and spices. Along the Rajaji Salai (formerly Beach Road) and the Kamarajar Promenade there are still some of Chennai's most striking colonial architecture. Buildings such as the Madras High Court, Chepauk Palace, and Madras University's Senate House feature what is known as the Indo-Saracenic style, which combined British architecture with domes, arches, and ornaments from Indian and Islamic building traditions. The red façades and the monumental dimensions in particular highlight the importance of Madras for the British colonial power at that time. (© Shutterstock) Also Worth Seeing: Marina Beach: At around 13 kilometers (8 miles) long, Marina Beach is one of the longest urban beaches in the world. Unfortunately, bathing is strictly forbidden and life-threatening here due to extremely strong undercurrents. Fort St. George: This fort was built in 1644 by the British East India Company and marked the beginning of British colonial rule in southern India. Particularly worth seeing are the massive white colonial buildings, old cannon emplacements, and St. Mary's Church, which is considered the oldest Anglican church in India. This area shows how strongly Chennai – then still Madras – was shaped by trade and colonial politics. Government Museum Chennai: This museum is one of the oldest in India and has one of the most significant collections of bronze sculptures from the time of the Chola dynasty, which dominated the South Indian region from the 9th to the 13th century. Many figures show Hindu deities with exceptionally fine details and are considered to be historic highlights of Tamil temple art. If there is one thing that is typical of India, it is the small auto rickshaws that speed through the streets. The three-wheeled vehicles are also part of the cityscape in Chennai. A drive through Mylapore or along Marina Beach is characterized by rapidly changing or overlapping sensory impressions: by the roar of traffic including continuous honking and temple music, by the scent of spices and the sea, by colorful street stalls and imposing buildings. (© Shutterstock) More Experiences: Walk through Mylapore: This district is one of the oldest in Chennai. Jasmine stalls, small temples, narrow side streets, and traditional residential buildings still convey much about how Chennai used to be away from the modern business districts. The Kapaleeshwarar Temple is also located in the middle of Mylapore and characterizes the district with its colorful tower, the surrounding flower stalls, incense, and the prayers of its visitors. Experience Tamil cinema at Rohini Silver Screens: Chennai is considered the heart of the Tamil film industry Kollywood. Before big film releases, fans often gather in front of the cinemas at night, hang meter-high banners, and celebrate the star actors as if they were pop icons. Attend a classical Bharatanatyam dance performance: Chennai is considered one of the major centers of Bharatanatyam, the classical dance of South India. Precise foot rhythms, expressive hand movements, and traditional music tell stories from Hindu epics and Tamil culture and can be experienced at venues such as the academy of the Kalakshetra Foundation. Between Motown, industrial history, and a surprisingly vibrant creative scene, there are plenty of culinary delights, interesting sights, and exciting activities to be found here Round peg in a square hole: that’s the essence of the invention of Detroit-style pizza. To set himself apart from other Detroit pizzerias, restaurateur Gus Guerra baked a Sicilian-inspired pizza in a square blue steel pan at his restaurant Buddy’s Rendezvous in 1946. The pans were originally used as drip trays or parts containers in the automotive industry, but when used for baking, they have an unexpected effect on the flavor: the even distribution of heat ensures a crisp base, a light, focaccia-like crust, and caramelized cheese edges. This comfort food is topped in reverse order: first are the toppings such as pepperoni, then a thick layer of traditional Wisconsin brick cheese, and finally the tomato sauce on top. The most authentic way to enjoy “The Square” is where it all began: at Buddy’s Pizza. (© Mauritius; header image © Shutterstock) More Culinary Delights: Coney Dog: In Detroit, the Coney Dog is far more than just a hot dog – it is as much a part of the city’s history as factory buildings and immigrant culture. Unlike a typical hot dog, it is served with the characteristic Coney sauce, yellow mustard, and diced onions. In Downtown, two institutions – American Coney Island and Lafayette Coney Island – have been competing side by side for over a century, keeping the tradition alive to this day. Detroit City Distillery in Eastern Market: In Eastern Market, Detroit’s food culture meets a scene that values handmade products. Detroit City Distillery has been running a tasting room there since 2014 and operates as a local distillery with a classic range from gin to whisky. The paintings encircle what is known as Rivera Court like a panoramic view and literally draw you into the logic of production, from the interplay of tools to the precise sequences of movement of the machinery. Guardian Building: Anyone wishing to experience Detroit’s architecture should start in the lobby of the Guardian Building. With its Pewabic tiles, mosaics, and warm colors, this Art Deco skyscraper from 1929 reflects the optimism of a city that believed in growth at the time. The interior feels more like a cathedral of modernism than an office block. The Henry Ford Museum of American Innovation: Just a few miles west of Detroit lies one of the largest history museums in the USA. The Henry Ford Museum tells the story of American industry and innovation through original artefacts – from the Ford Model T and historic locomotives to the limousine of former President John F. Kennedy. The museum vividly illustrates just how closely Detroit is linked to the country’s industrial development. Like a slice through Detroit: This is the impression given by the Dequindre Cut, a sunken cycle route and footpath that winds its way through the city. The almost two-mile-long greenway follows a former railway line, connecting Eastern Market with the Detroit Riverfront, and is ideal for walking, jogging, or a leisurely cycle ride away from the traffic. Amid brick walls, bridges, and street art, down here you experience a unique, tranquil energy while the city carries on as usual up above. At the end, the path opens out onto the riverside promenade, and Detroit suddenly reveals itself as vast, bright, and surprisingly nautical.
